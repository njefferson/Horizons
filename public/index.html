                                            What Quietkeep is

                                            Quietkeep holds everything you put down and brings it back on its own — so you do not have to hold it in your head, or remember to look. It is a planner that lives entirely on your device: no account, no sign-in, and no server holding your writing, because there is no server.

                                            Quietkeep holds everything you put down and brings it back on its own — so you do not have to hold it in your head, or remember to look. It is a planner that lives on your devices: no account, no sign-in, and nothing readable ever leaves them — what your devices trade is sealed with a key only they hold.

                                            What it is not

                                            It is not a medical or therapeutic tool, and nothing in it is advice. It never scores you. It cannot reach you on its own, so reminders go through your calendar. It holds only what you put in.

                                            How to use it

                                            Three things, and the app does the rest:

                                            • Put it down the moment it occurs to you — one box, nothing to fill in, no folder to choose.
                                            • Sort it when you have a moment — Quietkeep walks you through what you dumped, one card at a time, with a choice you cannot get wrong.
                                            • Do the next thing — it hands you the single thing worth doing now, and quietly brings each item back when its time comes.

                                            You never file anything or set reminders by hand. Dates, repeats and nudges are the app’s job, not yours.

                                            Add it to your home screen

                                            Quietkeep runs in the browser, but it is better on your home screen: it opens like an app, works with no signal, and it is where the browser can hold onto your planner for good.

                                            • iPhone or iPad (Safari): tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
                                            • Android or a computer (Chrome / Edge): open the browser menu and choose Install, or use the install icon in the address bar.

                                            Short answers to the things people ask. Tap one to open it.

                                            How do I get something out of my head?

                                            Type it in the box at the top and press Hold it. One line is enough — you are only getting it down, not sorting it. Do that as often as things occur to you; sorting comes later, on your terms.

                                            What happens to everything I put in?

                                            When you have a moment, Quietkeep offers each one back to you, one at a time, with a small choice: a thing to do, something you are waiting on someone else for, or just a note to keep. Nothing piles up unsorted where you cannot see it.

                                            How does it decide what to show me next?

                                            It hands you a single next thing — the one most worth doing now — with a short list behind it, never the whole pile at once. If it is not the right moment for that one, you can pass, and passing costs nothing.

                                            What if a date has already gone by?

                                            Nothing here is ever marked late or overdue — that is deliberate. A date that has passed quietly becomes a card asking when you would like to look at it again. There is no pile of failures to face.

                                            Will it remind me?

                                            Quietkeep does not send notifications. To be reminded at a set time, send that item to your calendar with “Send to my calendar” below — your calendar runs even when this app is closed, so it is the thing that can actually reach you.

                                            Is my writing private? Where does it live?

                                            Everything stays on this device. No account, no sign-in, and no server holding your writing, because there is no server. Nothing about you is collected.

                                            Everything stays on this device — no account, no sign-in. When your devices trade changes, the copy that travels is sealed here first, with a key only your devices hold; the relay it passes through cannot read a word of it. Nothing about you is collected.

                                            How do I back it up, or move to a new device?

                                            “Export a copy” under Your data saves everything as one plain file. On another device, “Bringing a copy back” reads that file and tells you what is in it before anything changes.

                                            Can I keep two devices in step?

                                            There is a separate edition, Quietkeep Sync, that does exactly that — sealed on your device before anything leaves, with a key only your devices hold. The default app you are in never contacts anything at all.

                                            Yes — this is Quietkeep Sync, the edition that does exactly that: sealed on this device before anything leaves, with a key only your devices hold. Pairing lives under “Keeping two devices in step” in Your data.

                                            Why does it work this way?

                                            Every choice here — bringing things back instead of listing them, never marking anything overdue, one next thing rather than the whole pile — comes from research on how memory, attention and motivation actually work, and how that differs for a lot of neurodivergent people. The full reasoning, with every source named and tagged by how well established it is, is written up in Planning for Humans, here in the app.

                                            Bringing a copy back

                                            Moving to a new device, or restoring after something went wrong. Choose an exported file and Quietkeep will tell you what is in it before anything changes.

                                            Take in what I don’t have adds anything this copy has and this device doesn’t, and removes nothing — that is the one for carrying your work between your own devices. Replace everything throws away what is here and uses the copy instead — that is the one for a device you are setting up again.

                                            The record itself

                                            Everything Quietkeep shows you is worked out fresh from one append-only record of what happened. You can read that record — every line, in plain words, newest day first. Looking changes nothing.

                                              Things you let go

                                              Letting go is a decision, and decisions can be revisited. Everything you let go is here, newest first — open one and “Keep it after all” brings it back with a clock of its own.

                                                The Not Now ledger

                                                Things other people asked of you, that you decided not to carry. Each is parked — it comes back on its own day, quietly — and each is kept, so when the same request comes back you can point at the decision instead of making it again.

                                                  The journal

                                                  Somewhere to write that is not a list and asks nothing of you. Entries are scrambled with a passphrase only you know, so they cannot be read from this device’s storage, from a backup, or by anyone Quietkeep syncs through. Nothing here is ever counted, searched, or brought back at you.

                                                  It stays scrambled until you open the journal again.

                                                    One pass through your messages

                                                    Messages arrive all day and attention does not divide. If you turn this on, Quietkeep offers a single pass through them at the moment you come out of working on something — when looking costs least — and never while you are in the middle of anything.

                                                    It is off unless you ask for it, it counts nothing, and skipping one is not a failure. Quietkeep cannot see your messages and never will.

                                                    Composing your day, by hand

                                                    If you turn this on, anything you hold can be chosen for today from its own sheet — up to five — and the chosen few sit quietly above Next up. The app never picks for you, and Next up’s own offer is unchanged.

                                                    It is off unless you ask for it, it counts nothing, and an unfinished choice is not a failure — at midnight the choosing simply lapses, with no record of a miss.

                                                    A day for requests

                                                    If you choose a day, anything someone asks of you can be parked to wait for it from its own sheet — so a request is dealt with on your day, not at the moment it lands. Declining sends things to the same day.

                                                    It is off unless you choose a day, it counts nothing, and nothing arrives on its own — a waiting request comes back the way any parked thing does, quietly, when its day ends.

                                                    How long a timer runs

                                                    When you send something to Do now you can start a timer on it. This is how long it runs. Choose it here, calmly — not in the moment you are trying to start something.

                                                    The time is something you commit to, not something you are held to. While it runs the app shows only that it is running — no countdown, no bar filling up — because a shape that is part-way full is a way of saying you stopped short. Leaving early records nothing at all, and when the time is up the timer simply goes.

                                                    Today, on paper

                                                    One page: the thing to do next, what else is ready, what is with other people, and what is coming up. For a meeting where a screen is rude, or a day the battery is going to lose.

                                                    Paper cannot update. Ticking something off on the printout does not reach Quietkeep — the app is still where things are true.

                                                    Telling someone where things are

                                                    What has changed since the last time you told anyone — finished, come back, now with someone else, and what was decided — plus what is still outstanding and what is coming up. It is worked out from your own history, so nothing has to be kept up to date for it to be right.

                                                    Nothing is sent anywhere. It is written for you to paste or hand over yourself, and the next one starts from this moment.

                                                    Since when

                                                    By default a report covers everything since the last one you handed over. If you have a meeting that sets the rhythm — a staff call, a catch-up — you can name it and say when it came round, and the report will cover the time since then instead.

                                                      Nothing here asks anything of you. A period is a name for a stretch of time, not a thing to do — it takes no date, it never comes back at you, and Quietkeep does not count how often you mark it.

                                                      Trying it out with something in it
                                                      
  
                                                      
    An empty planner is hard to judge. This puts a small set of ordinary work in,
    dated around today — a job with two steps in it, something whose date has
    already gone by, something you are waiting on someone else for, a couple of
    things nobody is asking you to do, and two notes not yet sorted.
  
                                                      
  
                                                      
    
  
                                                      
  
                                                      
  
                                                      
    It goes in beside anything you already have and behaves exactly like your own
    work — there is no button that takes only this back out again. Export a
    copy first if what is already here matters.
  
                                                      

  
  
                                                      A whole invented life, to try things on
                                                      
  
                                                      
    This makes a file — hundreds of invented things, dated around today, with
    something of every kind the app has in it: jobs with steps under them, dates
    that have gone by, people you are waiting on, things folded together as
    duplicates, things you let go, weights, a sealed journal. It is for seeing how
    the app behaves with a full life in it rather than an empty one.
  
                                                      
  
                                                      
    
  
                                                      
  
                                                      
  
                                                      
    Nothing here changes what is on this device. The file only becomes real work
    when you bring it back in with “Bring a copy back” above —
    and for trying this set on, the door you want there is “Replace
    everything”, which swaps this device’s work for the file. The
    other door, “Take in what I don’t have”, adds the
    invented things beside your real ones instead — and there is no button
    that takes just those back out again. Take a copy of your own first, and
    replace with that copy when you have finished looking.
