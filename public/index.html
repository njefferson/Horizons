                      What Quietkeep is

                      Quietkeep holds everything you put down and brings it back on its own — so you do not have to hold it in your head, or remember to look. It is a planner that lives entirely on your device: no account, no sign-in, and no server holding your writing, because there is no server.

                      How to use it

                      Three things, and the app does the rest:

                      • Put it down the moment it occurs to you — one box, nothing to fill in, no folder to choose.
                      • Sort it when you have a moment — Quietkeep walks you through what you dumped, one card at a time, with a choice you cannot get wrong.
                      • Do the next thing — it hands you the single thing worth doing now, and quietly brings each item back when its time comes.

                      You never file anything or set reminders by hand. Dates, repeats and nudges are the app’s job, not yours.

                      Add it to your home screen

                      Quietkeep runs in the browser, but it is better on your home screen: it opens like an app, works with no signal, and it is where the browser can hold onto your planner for good.

                      • iPhone or iPad (Safari): tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
                      • Android or a computer (Chrome / Edge): open the browser menu and choose Install, or use the install icon in the address bar.

                      Extras

                      One pass through your messages

                      Messages arrive all day and attention does not divide. If you turn this on, Quietkeep offers a single pass through them at the moment you come out of working on something — when looking costs least — and never while you are in the middle of anything.

                      It is off unless you ask for it, it counts nothing, and skipping one is not a failure. Quietkeep cannot see your messages and never will.

                      Today, on paper

                      One page: the thing to do next, what else is ready, what is with other people, and what is coming up. For a meeting where a screen is rude, or a day the battery is going to lose.

                      Paper cannot update. Ticking something off on the printout does not reach Quietkeep — the app is still where things are true.

                      Telling someone where things are

                      What has changed since the last time you told anyone — finished, come back, now with someone else — plus what is still outstanding and what is coming up. It is worked out from your own history, so nothing has to be kept up to date for it to be right.

                      Nothing is sent anywhere. It is written for you to paste or hand over yourself, and the next one starts from this moment.

                      Trying it out with something in it
                      
  
                      
    An empty planner is hard to judge. This puts a small set of ordinary work in,
    dated around today — a job with two steps in it, something whose date has
    already gone by, something you are waiting on someone else for, a couple of
    things nobody is asking you to do, and two notes not yet sorted.
  
                      
  
                      
    
  
                      
  
                      
  
                      
    It goes in beside anything you already have and behaves exactly like your own
    work — there is no button that takes only this back out again. Export a
    copy first if what is already here matters.
  
                      

  
                      Bringing work in from another planner
                      
  
                      
    An OmniFocus export — TaskPaper or CSV — or anything else
    TaskPaper-shaped. Projects keep their contents, and dates you set over there
    arrive as dates you set here.
  
                      
  
  
  
                      
  
                      
    
  
                      
  
                      
    It goes in beside whatever is already here. If you want a clean run at
    it, save a copy and start again from empty first — both are just below.

    Two different things, and they are not the same favour. Save a copy first
